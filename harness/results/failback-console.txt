── STEP 1 ── Confirm which node is actually master right now
     n65-n2 : active
     n65-n1 : standby

── STEP 2 ── Check that the target has applied everything it has been sent
     db_ha_apply_info on n65-n1 (eof / final / lag / fail): <none>
     apply lag = ? log pages   fail_counter = ?
     NOTE: this number is blind to a COPY stall. eof_lsa is how far copylogdb has
           FETCHED, so if copying is stuck, eof stops moving and lag reads 0 while
           the target is arbitrarily far behind the master (measured, OQ7 phase 4).
           The only view that sees it is: cubrid applyinfo -L <logpath> -r n65-n2 -a -i 1 hadb

  ?? DECISION ─────────────────────────────────────────────
     Is the target caught up enough to take over?
     · A non-zero lag means the writes not yet applied are lost when n65-n2 steps down.
     · A non-zero fail_counter means replication is BROKEN, not merely behind, and
     · failing back onto it will not fix itself.
     · We do not know your threshold. Zero? A page? A second of business time?
     default: y
     --auto: taking the default

── STEP 3 ── Decide what happens to the application during the switch
     Stopping the heartbeat on n65-n2 takes its SERVER down with it -- clients on
     n65-n2 are disconnected, and there is a window with no master at all.

  ?? DECISION ─────────────────────────────────────────────
     Has write traffic been quiesced / drained?
     · CUBRID has no read-only mode to hold a master still while replication catches up.
     · Anything committed on n65-n2 after the lag reading above is at risk.
     · How do you actually do this -- connection draining at the broker, at the app,
     · or do you simply accept the loss?
     default: y
     --auto: taking the default

── STEP 4 ── Stop the heartbeat on the current master (n65-n2)
     This is the switch. With n65-n2 out of the group, n65-n1 is the only node left
     and the heartbeat promotes it.

  ?? DECISION ─────────────────────────────────────────────
     Run: cubrid heartbeat stop on n65-n2 ?
     · There is no supported way to demote a master in place: changemode refuses an
     · active->standby transition the heartbeat did not drive (server_support.c:1558).
     · If you use a different mechanism -- deregister, service stop, killing cub_master --
     · tell us which and why.
     default: y
     --auto: taking the default
     (bounded at 180 s: this command can hang forever if the node's HA processes
      become zombies -- us_hb_deactivate polls "is any cub_server running" and a
      zombie answers yes. Measured 2026-08-27 in a container without a reaping PID 1.)
     @ cubrid heartbeat stop
     ++ cubrid heartbeat stop: success

── STEP 5 ── Wait for the target to be promoted
     n65-n1 is now: active (after 2s)

── STEP 6 ── Bring the old master back as a slave
     TRAP: after 'heartbeat stop', 'heartbeat start' alone fails with
           "CUBRID heartbeat feature is being deactivated". A full service
           stop/start is required first (measured in the CBRD-26983 session).

  ?? DECISION ─────────────────────────────────────────────
     Run: cubrid service stop; cubrid service start; on n65-n2 ?
     · If n65-n2's log diverged -- it accepted writes n65-n1 never received -- rejoining
     · may not be possible at all, and it needs a fresh backupdb/restoreslave instead.
     · Do you check for divergence before rejoining, and how?
     default: y
     --auto: taking the default
     cubrid manager server is not installed.
     @ cubrid master stop
     ++ cubrid master stop: success
     ++ cubrid broker start: success
     @ cubrid manager server start
     cubrid manager server is not installed.
     ++ applylogdb start: success
     ++ HA processes start: success
     ++ cubrid heartbeat start: success

── STEP 7 ── Verify the cluster
     n65-n1 : active   n65-n2 : standby
     replication on n65-n2 (eof / final / lag / fail):
